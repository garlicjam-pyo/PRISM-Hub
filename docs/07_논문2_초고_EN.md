# Paper 2 working draft — revised after independent review, 2026-09-22

**Title.** Causal Load Admission Under Power and Observation Constraints in a Partial-Power Vehicle Hub

**Status.** Research draft. The previous claims of robust 15 ms LP superiority and elimination of bus collapse are withdrawn. This draft records tested limitations and a static admission component; a full dynamic scheduling study is still required.

**Abstract.** Load admission for a partial-power converter must distinguish regulated-load power from series-path and DAB link currents. It must also account for physical events that can precede command observation. A reproduced counterexample to an observation-anchored window produces 122.161 A for 10 ms before the controller receives a command, within the assumed delay bounds. A candidate admission function instead derives a static budget from the converter's power balance, port constraints, and 25 kW continuous rating. With 5% reserve at 400 V, the budget is 59.375 A. It returns an explicit infeasibility state when event coverage is unavailable or mandatory demand alone exceeds this budget. Priority allocation is equivalent to a one-step linear program; a longer separable horizon provides no demonstrated benefit. The results motivate an explicit producer-approval or permanent-reserve contract and a common-plant dynamic evaluation. They do not establish voltage safety or algorithmic novelty.

## I. Prior work and research question

Vehicle priority-based load admission is established. [JLR US20190023203A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20190023203A1/en) describes available-current checks and disabling or inhibiting loads when a converter saturates. Thus neither priority admission nor converter-capacity awareness alone is a new contribution. The [independent review](reviews/2026-09-21/sals_review.md) records additional relevant scheduling and reference-governor literature.

The research question is whether an explicitly modeled PPRC constraint set and causal event contract can improve a measured service/voltage tradeoff relative to established policies. No superiority result is currently available.

## II. Plant and continuous budget

The candidate plant is the finite-link, six-state model in Paper 1. Continuous load is capped at 25 kW, independently of the DAB link's 8 kW rating and the path's 100 A rating. A static feasibility calculation checks series voltage, path/reference current, bridge modulation, DAB link current, and SPS power capability. A 5% margin yields at most 23.75 kW, or 59.375 A at 400 V. This current-equivalent representation applies at the nominal bus voltage and is not a dynamic stability certificate for constant-power loads.

## III. Observation contract and counterexample

In the historical synthetic scenario, a compressor command occurs at 50 ms, the physical surge starts at 70 ms, and observation occurs at 80 ms. The allowed 20 ms physical delay is shorter than the allowed 30 ms communication delay. Moving the window start to observation leaves a 10 ms blind interval.

During that interval, base demand 14.9 A, compressor demand 24.9×2.39 A, V2L demand 9 A, and admitted deferrable demand 38.75 A sum to 122.161 A. This is reproduced by calling the imported v0.9 Monte Carlo scheduler, without changing its allocation logic. Zero failures in a finite random sample cannot exclude this case.

A valid coverage contract must be established outside the allocation function: for example, the producer waits for admission before starting, or sufficient reserve is held even before observation. A timestamp received after an event cannot retrospectively protect the bus. A failed contract causes the prototype to disable deferrable admission and explicitly refuse a safety/feasibility claim; mandatory loads still need a separate response.

## IV. Current implementation

`python/sals_admission.py` takes present requests, priorities, a mandatory-demand upper bound, battery voltage, and an explicit coverage-valid flag. It computes the static budget and allocates remaining capacity in descending priority order. This solves the tested one-step linear program. No future request or actual future delay is used.

If the mandatory upper bound exceeds the budget, the function reports the deficit and zero deferrable allowance. In the historical scenario the bound is 91.16 A, exceeding 59.375 A by 31.785 A. No scheduler acting only on deferrable loads can satisfy that bound. The producer contract, mandatory-load classification, or hardware rating must change.

## V. Evidence and missing comparisons

[The regression](../python/validate_sals_contract.py) checks the blind-interval counterexample, greedy/LP equivalence, missing-coverage behavior, mandatory infeasibility, and unmet demand integrated in A·ms. First-full-admission delay alone omits later curtailment. The historic Monte Carlo remains a current-based surrogate; it is not a new finite-link voltage simulation.

A completed study must compare no shaping, reactive shedding, one-step priority admission, window-based admission, and any time-coupled extension on the same nonlinear plant, actuator limits, uncertainty set, and mandatory-load contract. Report voltage/port violations, unserved energy, longest interruption, minimum service, and measured WCET. Any hardware alternative must also be consistently sized. The learned predictor's historical negative result applies to the tested synthetic signals and does not establish that learning or MPC cannot help other designs.

## VI. Provisional conclusion

The tested development result is identification of two missing constraints: the converter's actual load-power budget and a causal contract covering events before observation. The added static admission code makes their violation explicit. Demonstrating improved closed-loop performance or a distinct research contribution remains future work.
