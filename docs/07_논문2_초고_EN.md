# Paper 2 draft (v0.1, 2026-09-21) — for IEEE Trans. Transportation Electrification / Industrial Electronics

**Title.** Saturation-Aware Anticipatory Load Shaping: Worst-Case-Window Scheduling of Vehicle Loads to Protect a Sub-Rated Series Partial-Power Regulator

**Abstract.** Partial-power regulation lets an on-board DC bus be held by a cell rated at a few percent of the bulk power, but the cell's current rating then becomes a hard ceiling that no fast control can move: in a verified 800 V hub (Paper 1) a 100 A series cell rides through a 60 A step with 4.7 V droop, yet any step beyond the 87.5 A headroom collapses the bus regardless of feedforward, pre-boost or predictive control. This paper turns the problem around: instead of enlarging the cell, the vehicle's own anticipatory signals (compressor and heater commands, V2L requests) are used to schedule deferrable loads so that the predicted total never exceeds the headroom. The key finding is *what kind* of prediction works. A learned quantile predictor trained on synthetic CAN data is no better than persistence (MAE 1.4 vs 1.3 A) because the command-to-surge delay of a compressor is broadly distributed (20–200 ms), and even its 90 % upper bound misses the surge and the bus collapses in closed loop. A worst-case-window representation—"a surge of 2.2× may occur anywhere in [+20, +220] ms after the command"—fed to a 1 ms linear program survives every case. In a cold-start event peaking at 110 A the scheduler keeps the bus at 396 V with the 100 A cell, matching a 150 A hardware alternative, while a reactive under-voltage load dump dips 12.5 %. Over 300 Monte-Carlo episodes (battery 640–920 V, delay 20–200 ms, surge 1.6–2.4×, CAN loss ≤20 %) rating violations occur in 25 % of episodes without shaping and in none with it; deferrals are 1 ms median and ≤200 ms maximum. The scheduler is rule-based and solvable within the 1 ms period; learning is confined to narrowing the window when delay-determining signals exist.

**Index terms.** Load management, partial power processing, DC bus, electric vehicle, robust scheduling, linear programming.

## I. Introduction
- Sub-rated regulation cells (5 % of bulk power) and the headroom cliff (Paper 1, Fig. 4).
- Fast-scale options exhausted: PI (1.8 kHz), state feedback (10 kHz, 4.7 V), measured/predicted feedforward (2.1–4.7 V), pre-boost (0 V but +8 V bias), MPC (cannot beat the 15 µs actuator delay). None move the 87.5 A cliff.
- Slow-scale option: the loads are commanded by the vehicle before they draw current; the delay is known only as a range.
- Contributions: (1) formulation of load shaping as a headroom-constrained LP over a 15 ms horizon at 1 ms; (2) the negative result on point/quantile prediction; (3) the worst-case-window rule and its Monte-Carlo survival; (4) comparison with reactive shedding and with hardware over-rating.

## II. Plant and headroom model
- Series path averaged model (state feedback, DAB lag 10 µs + delay 5 µs, |i_DAB| ≤ 100 A). Verified droop table: 25/60/80/87/95 A → 2.0/4.7/6.3/14.6/collapse.
- Headroom I_max = min(95 A, 0.95·8 kW/|400 − V_bat/2|); below 640 V this derates automatically.

## III. Loads and anticipatory signals
- Non-deferrable: base 10–15 A, heat-pump compressor (command → delay U(20,200) ms → surge 1.6–2.4× for 20 ms → ramp τ 50 ms), V2L (+5 ms), suspension ±7.5 A (5 ms).
- Deferrable with priorities: cabin PTC 17.5 A (1.0), battery heater 17.5 A (0.7), 48 V low-priority 3.75 A (0.5).
- CAN model: 10 ms sample-hold, 1–5 ms delay, loss ≤ 20 % (observed up to 30 ms late).

## IV. Scheduler
- Variables u_{j,k} ∈ [0, r_j], k = 1..15; minimize Σ w_j (r_j − u_{j,k})(1 + 0.02k); s.t. n̂_k + Σ_j u_{j,k} ≤ I_max. First move applied; HiGHS/linprog, 45 variables.
- Prediction n̂_k: (a) point (median GBM), (b) quantile 0.9 GBM, (c) worst-case window.
- Backup: reactive under-voltage dump at 388 V (shed all after 1 ms, restore every 20 ms), retained as supervisor.

## V. Results
- A. Cold-start event (Fig. 1 = `tbs6_sals.png`): table of bus minimum / deferral for none, reactive, 150 A hardware, SALS perfect, SALS point+errors, SALS window.
- B. Learned predictor (Fig. 2 = `wp45_predictor.png`): MAE table vs persistence; q0.9 coverage 87–90 % but max under-prediction 36–47 A; closed-loop collapse (Fig. 3 = `wp45_closed_loop.png`).
- C. Window rule vs actual delay 20/100/190 ms: bus ≥ 396 V in all.
- D. Monte Carlo (Fig. 4 = `sals_montecarlo.png`): 300 episodes; violations 25 % → 0; deferral median 1 ms, 95th 98–173 ms, max 199 ms; reactive dips median 22 V, max 58 V.
- E. Sensitivity: margin I_max 85–98 A, window width, surge factor bound.

## VI. Discussion
- Why learning fails here: information content of the command signal at 5–15 ms horizons is nil when the delay spread exceeds the horizon; set-valued prediction is the right object. Conditions under which learning helps: inverter ramp-state broadcast (delay becomes known), online identification of window width and surge factor.
- Cost of robustness: deferral of comfort loads by ≤200 ms; no hardware cost. Comparison with over-rating (+50 % series-cell silicon and transformer).
- Relation to prior work: auxiliary-load smoothing for battery stress [IEEE conf. 2020, Xplore 9160762], reactive converter-overload handling [US 9371005]; neither uses converter headroom as the constraint or a worst-case-window LP.

## VII. Conclusion
The converter rating, not the control bandwidth, limits transient survival of a partial-power bus; scheduling the vehicle's deferrable loads against a worst-case window of the non-deferrable ones removes the limit without hardware, and this must be done with set-valued, not point, prediction.
