**Title.** Energy-Consistent Averaged Modeling of a Two-Stage Series Regulator for an 800 V Vehicle Power Hub

**Status.** Research draft, not submission-ready. This version replaces unsupported full-system validation and novelty claims in v0.1. The original draft remains in Git history. Reproducible evidence is in [the integration validation set](../validation/2026-09-22-integration/README.md).

**Abstract.** A candidate vehicle power hub combines a fixed-ratio resonant switched-capacitor bulk path with a series voltage regulator. A two-stage regulator, comprising an isolated DAB, a finite 100 V link, and an H-bridge with an output filter, is modeled while accounting for power drawn from the regulated bus. Separating path, bridge, and link currents reveals a limitation hidden by a virtual current-source actuator: a 100 A reference limit can produce a 106.92 A path-current peak even when bus voltage recovers. A candidate 84 A reference limit meets the tested voltage and port criteria for 5-to-25 kW steps at five battery voltages from 640 to 920 V, with bus droop of 3.69–5.08 V. Independent MATLAB and Python integrations agree to numerical precision using identical control gains. Additional unloading, regeneration, and series-voltage zero-crossing cases are reported, along with deliberate overload failures. These results establish consistency and sampled operating-point feasibility of an averaged candidate. Switching behavior, passive-component sizing, protection, charging-mode integration, and hardware superiority remain unverified.

## I. Scope and prior work

Fixed-ratio switched-tank conversion combined with partial-power regulation is established; see [He et al., APEC 2018](https://research.google/pubs/switched-tank-converter-based-partial-power-architecture-for-voltage-regulation-applications/) and the [prior-art review](reviews/2026-09-21/hardware_prior_art.md). Bidirectional step-up/down series PPCs also require comparison with [Hassanpour et al., TPEL 2023](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPEL.2023.3289061). The present numerical work does not establish a unique topology.

The attachment incorrectly attributed US10363828B1 and US10632863B2 to GM. Their original assignee is NIO USA. [US10632863B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10632863B2/en) includes a specific resonant switching arrangement and battery configuration; reversing the headline power-flow direction alone does not establish distinctness. [Karma US11235676B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11235676B2/en) is relevant to combined conversion and auxiliary power integration. No exhaustive search or freedom-to-operate conclusion is claimed.

## II. Architecture and rating definitions

Stage A is a proposed 2:1 resonant switched-capacitor bulk path. A charger connection at its output node V_A is the current architecture candidate. A separate series regulator supplies the regulated auxiliary bus; the earlier charge-bypass switch proposal is superseded. Simultaneous charging and auxiliary-bus regulation has not been integrated or tested in this model.

The regulator contains an 8 kW DAB rated at the link port, a floating 100 V link, and a PWM H-bridge/filter producing a signed series voltage. Link current is limited by 80 A, SPS capability, and link-port power. The path and bridge are evaluated against 100 A. These values are not interchangeable with the 25 kW/400 V load current of 62.5 A.

The new link capacitor (2 mF) and bridge filter (5 µH, 2 mΩ) are provisional values. A 5 kW Stage C is proposed for simultaneous 48 V/3 kW and 12 V/1.5 kW loads; the existing 3 kW implementation does not validate that proposal.

## III. Averaged equations and control

The six states are path current, series voltage, bus voltage, bridge-filter current, link voltage, and DAB link current. The full equations, signs, and energy identity are given in [the development report](10_검토대응_통합개발.md). DAB primary power is P_link/0.96 in forward transfer and 0.96 P_link in reverse transfer. The link capacitor and bridge inductor store energy explicitly. A sampled bus controller designed from a reduced plant and a link-voltage PI controller drive bounded modulation and link-current commands.

The bus pole-placement frequency parameter is 4 kHz; it is not a measured full-plant bandwidth. Sample time and transport delay are each 5 µs. Actual state variables are never clipped to pass a rating check. Finite actuator lag can therefore produce a reported violation even after a command is limited.

At 648 V and 25 kW, path current is 78.224 A, series voltage 77.033 V, link current 60.380 A, link power 6.038 kW, and DAB primary power 6.290 kW. The earlier 19% processing estimate used the load current instead of the circulating path current and is withdrawn. Below the voltage-reach boundary, reducing load alone cannot restore a 400 V target.

The five rated tests use a candidate 84 A reference limit. Further passing cases comprise two load removals, two regeneration transitions, and one series-voltage zero crossing. All start from an energized steady state and run for 6 ms; they are not startup or fault-protection tests. Midpoint integration uses 0.2 µs. Halving the step changes the rated 640 V bus waveform by at most 0.000391 V. MATLAB independently solves the equilibrium and integrates the plant with the same exported gains; maximum scalar-metric disagreement is 1.91×10⁻¹⁰.

Stage A bidirectional ZCS regression and the ideal charger's 150 kW/375 A envelope are separately tested in [the previous validation](../validation/2026-09-22/README.md). They do not establish the efficiency or simultaneous-mode performance of the complete hub.

## V. Claims withheld and completion criteria

The earlier 4.5-to-0.5 kW loss reduction, 180-to-12 kVA magnetic comparison, universal 60 A transient claim, and “MATLAB-verified complete system” statement are not retained as demonstrated results. A fair comparison must include the link capacitor, H-bridge/filter, cooling, protection, Stage C rating, and equivalent boost/DCX/APM alternatives.

Required next evidence includes a switching schematic and simulation, device RMS/peak stresses and soft switching, passive-component selection, uncertainty and timing sweeps, charging-port transitions, and Stage C closed-loop operation. Novelty must be established by a circuit- and control-level comparison after that definition is complete.
