# Paper 1 draft (v0.1, 2026-09-21) — for IEEE Trans. Transportation Electrification / Power Electronics

**Title.** A 400/800 V-Compatible On-Board DC Power Hub Using a Fixed-Ratio Resonant Switched-Capacitor Array with Series Partial-Power Regulation and Fixed-Gain Multiport CLLC Stages

**Abstract.** 800 V battery platforms must still draw fast-charge power from the installed base of 400 V-class chargers and must feed 400 V, 48 V and 12 V on-board loads. Today this is done with a full-power boost converter plus a separate wide-input auxiliary power module, which costs 4–5 kW of loss during 150 kW charging and a 150 kVA-class magnetic. This paper proposes PRISM-Hub, a single on-board hub in which (i) an eight-phase 2:1 resonant switched-capacitor (RSC) array carries the full 150 kW path at a fixed ratio with zero-current switching, (ii) an 8 kW isolated dual-active-bridge (DAB) cell inserted in series regulates the 400 V bus over the 640–920 V battery range while processing at most 19 % of the auxiliary power, and is bypassed during 400 V charging because the 2:1 ratio alone places the charger terminal within 331–460 V, and (iii) a fixed-gain 25:3 CLLC stage supplies a 48 V zonal bus with a 48→12 V buck for legacy loads. Design rules obtained from switching-level simulation are reported: the RSC zero-current window is only ±1 % of the resonant frequency, so a self-oscillating zero-crossing timing with a 0.80–1.12× blanking window is required (0/20 Monte-Carlo failures versus 17/20 for fixed timing); the output node must present ≥50× the flying capacitance; the series path forms a Q≈10 resonance with the bus capacitors that limits a PI regulator to 1.8 kHz, whereas state feedback with integral action achieves 10 kHz and 4.7 V (1.2 %) droop for a 60 A step; and auxiliary power must be derated below 640 V. Compared with the two-converter baseline, the charging-path loss falls from 4.5 kW to 0.5 kW (99.67 %), magnetic VA from ≈180 kVA to ≈12 kVA, and driving-mode battery-to-400 V efficiency rises from 96.5 % to 98.4–99.6 %. All results are from MATLAB-verified switching-level and averaged models; the closest prior art (switched-tank partial-power regulators and combined converter/APM circuits) is discussed and the contribution is delimited to the mode-dependent role assignment, the charge-mode bypass and the design rules.

**Index terms.** Electric vehicle, 800 V, resonant switched-capacitor converter, partial power processing, CLLC, auxiliary power module, fast charging compatibility.

---

## I. Introduction

Battery voltages of 800 V class shorten fast-charging time and reduce harness mass, but two legacies remain: most public DC chargers are 400 V class (≈500 V maximum output), and on-board loads at 400 V (compressor, PTC heaters, V2L inverters), 48 V (zonal actuators, active suspension) and 12 V (safety) must still be served [1]–[3]. The industrial answers—a full-power DC booster between charger and pack, plus a separate wide-input APM [Preh-type architecture, 13]—process 100 % of the charging power through a regulated, magnetically coupled stage and 100 % of the auxiliary power through a wide-gain isolated stage. Neither is necessary.

Partial-power processing (PPP) [4], [8] and fixed-ratio resonant switched-capacitor (RSC/STC) converters [9]–[12] offer an alternative: let a fixed-ratio, magnetics-free stage carry the bulk power at ~99 %, and let a small regulated cell handle only the voltage *difference*. This combination has been demonstrated for 48 V data-center regulation [He et al., APEC 2018] and for a wide-range EV battery interface with a non-isolated buck regulator [Ni et al., APEC 2019], and its theoretical limits have recently been formalized [12]. It has not, to our knowledge, been applied as a *multi-mode on-board hub* whose regulated cell changes role with the operating mode and is bypassed entirely during 400 V charging, nor combined with fixed-gain multiport isolation for 48/12 V. A combined converter/APM circuit for 800 V packs does exist [EP 3987646], but it uses a transformer-interfaced buck/boost stage and regulates the full 400 V path.

Contributions: (1) the PRISM-Hub architecture and its five operating modes; (2) a numerical design of all three stages with explicit rationale for every parameter (sweeps over switching frequency, battery voltage, quality factor); (3) four design rules found only in switching-level simulation—ZCS timing window, output-node stiffness, series-path resonance and its control, and low-voltage derating; (4) a quantitative comparison with the two-converter baseline. Section II states the application and requirements, III the architecture, IV the design, V the verification, VI the comparison and prior-art delimitation, VII conclusions.

## II. Application and Requirements

A 216-series NMC pack (648/788/907 V at 3.0/3.65/4.2 V per cell, 920 V absolute) is chosen: 788 V is representative of "800 V", and 4.2 V × 216 / 2 = 454 V stays below the 500 V ceiling of 400 V-class CCS chargers, which is the enabling condition for a fixed 2:1 ratio. Requirements (Table I): 150 kW at 400 V charging (375 A charger side, 187.5 A pack side); a 400 V intermediate bus at ±2 % steady/±4 % transient feeding up to 25 kW of driving-mode auxiliaries (heat-pump compressor ≤6 kW, PTC ≤7 kW, V2L 3.6 kW, 48 V port 5 kW); a 48 V port of 3 kW bidirectional with ±3 kW transients; 12 V at 1.5 kW; a 60 A (24 kW) load step with ≤2 % droop and ≤1 ms recovery.

## III. Architecture and Operating Modes

Fig. 1 shows the hub. **Stage A** is a 2:1 RSC array: four 37.5 kW modules of two interleaved ladder cells each (eight phases, 22.5° spacing), 750 V SiC switches stressed at V_bat/2 ≤ 460 V (61 %), flying capacitor 7.8 µF, resonant inductor 144 nH, resonant frequency 150 kHz. It is open-loop; its output is V_A = V_bat/2 − I·R_out with R_out = 2.3 mΩ for the array. **Stage B** is the partial-power regulation cell (PPRC): an 8 kW DAB fed from the 400 V bus whose secondary full bridge (200 V GaN) is inserted in series between V_A and the bus, adding −59…+77 V so that the bus stays at 400 V over the pack range. Its output current equals the path current, rated 100 A. **Stage C** is a 25:3 CLLC at a fixed 250 kHz resonant frequency (gain 1, 48 V) with a four-phase 48→12 V buck.

Modes: M1 driving (one Stage A module active, PPRC regulating); M2 800 V charging (charger on the pack, hub supplies auxiliaries); M3 400 V charging (all modules active, **PPRC bypassed**, charger current loop regulates); M4 V2L/V2H; M5 48 V pre-charge. The bypass decision follows from II: with the terminal at V_bat/2 + I·R ∈ [331, 460] V the charger's own CC–CV loop suffices (verified in V-F), and it avoids sizing the series cell's transformer for 375 A.

## IV. Design

### A. Stage A
Per phase: I_ph = 46.9 A average, half-sine peak (π/2)·I_ph = 73.6 A, switch rms I_pk/2 = 36.8 A, capacitor rms 52 A. A frequency sweep (50–400 kHz, Table II) shows efficiency falling slowly (C_oss loss ∝ f) while the flying capacitor shrinks as 1/f; 150 kHz is the knee (7.8 µF/500 V film, air-core 144 nH). Loss per phase 59 W (conduction 32.5, C_oss 14.4, capacitor 4.1, busbar 4.1, inductor 2.7, gate 1.2): η = 99.69 % at full load, 99.72 % peak at 45 % load. Without pre-charge the inrush is 3.4 kA per phase (Z_r = 0.136 Ω); a 20 Ω resistor into a 100 µF input capacitor limits it to 15.6 A with 13.6 ms to 95 %. In driving mode one module is run (phase shedding): 3 kW at 99.0 % instead of 96.3 %.

### B. Stage B
Over 648–920 V at 25 kW the required series voltage is +77…−59 V and the processed power ≤4.8 kW (19 %); the cell is rated 8 kW, ±90 V, 100 A (margin 1.67×). DAB: 4:1, 200 kHz, L_s = 11.1 µH for 8 kW at 60° phase shift. Series conduction loss at 62.5 A: 7.8 W (200 V GaN, 1 mΩ per switch). Driving-mode battery-to-bus efficiency: 98.4/99.0/99.4/99.6 % at 3/5/10/25 kW (788 V), ≥97.7 % at 648 V. Below 640 V the series voltage exceeds 90 V; auxiliary power is derated to 20 kW (rule R4).

### C. Stage C
R_ac = 43.2 Ω; Q = 0.4, m = L_m/L_r = 6 → L_r = 11.0 µH, C_r = 36.8 nF, L_m = 66 µH, magnetizing peak 6.1 A (ZVS energy 1.2 mJ ≫ 32 µJ). Gain sensitivity ±1 % for ±3 % frequency deviation, so f_s = f_r is fixed and ±10 % frequency shift is reserved for current limiting. A 12 V winding would need 0.75 turns; a buck is used instead. Estimated efficiency 97.8 %.

### D. Bus and control
C_bus = 400 µF, C_ser = 100 µF, L_path = 1.5 µH: C_eff = 80 µF gives f_res = 14.5 kHz with Q ≈ 10. The PPRC controller is state feedback on [i_path, v_ser, v_bus, i_DAB] with integral action, poles placed at 8 kHz (ζ = 0.7); gains are generated by script.

## V. Verification (switching-level and averaged simulation)

**Rule R1 — ZCS timing window (Fig. 2).** At f_s = f_r the turn-off current is 9.2 % of the peak: the 100 ns dead time shortens conduction by 3 % of the half period and the output capacitor raises the effective resonance by ~1 %. The optimum is f_s ≈ 0.98 f_r, but the window is ±1 %. With ±5 % capacitor and ±10 % inductor tolerance, fixed timing fails 17 of 20 Monte-Carlo draws (turn-off up to 39 %, loss +12–15 %); a common-period zero-crossing turn-off passes but costs +10 % loss; a per-phase self-oscillating zero-crossing period passes all 20 at 59.4 W. A blanking window of 0.80–1.12× the nominal conduction time is mandatory: without it, near no-load the detector toggles and energy flows back to the input.

**Rule R2 — output-node stiffness.** With an output capacitance of 2.6× C_fly the effective resonance shifts 18 % and the peak rises to 81 A; ≥50× is required, so each module carries ≥100 µF local capacitance.

**Array (Fig. 3).** Eight phases with ±10 % tolerance share within ±10.5 % per phase and ±1.9 % per module; bus ripple 0.21 V; total loss 471 W (predicted 472 W).

**Rule R3 — series-path resonance and control (Fig. 4).** A PI regulator is limited to 1.8 kHz by the Q ≈ 10 resonance and gives 8.6 V droop with 430 µs recovery for a 60 A step. State feedback achieves 10 kHz for L_path = 0.5–3 µH and 4.7 V droop with 80 µs recovery; the DAB current saturates at 100 A, so steps above the headroom (87.5 A) collapse the bus—an actuator limit that feedforward cannot remove (Paper 2). Halving the capacitor bank yields 7.6 V; quartering is unstable because of the 15 µs actuator delay against a 29 kHz resonance.

**Device peak voltage.** The residual ZCS current cannot even charge the leg node to the rail (V_reach ≤ 386 V), so overshoot is caused only by the complementary turn-on edge exciting the package loop: with ≥20 ns edges and ≤10 nH loop inductance the peak stays ≤500 V (Fig. 5).

**CLLC (Fig. 6).** Gain 0.998 at f_r (FHA error ≤0.5 % over ±3 %), magnetizing current 5.9 A, ZVS margin 3.7–5.9×, rms 9.1/68.3 A, 97.85 %; input ±2 % gives 47.1–49.1 V. Bidirectional operation: with both bridges driven synchronously (φ = 0) the stage behaves as a DC transformer and reverses in 57.5 µs on a 2 V change of the 48 V OCV; phase-shift control is unsuitable at resonance (tens of kW circulate for a few degrees), and a 10 % frequency shift limits reverse current.

**Charging and transitions (Fig. 7).** 150 kW CC–CV through the array with the PPRC bypassed: terminal 331–460 V over SoC, CV reached by the charger loop alone, path efficiency 99.67 % including bypass. Bypass closing/opening perturbs the bus by 0.09/1.2 V with a 13.2 A current peak. 800 V charging with a 10 kW auxiliary step: 2.0 V. Regeneration reversal at the 48 V port: 0.6 V overshoot.

## VI. Comparison and Prior Art

Table III compares with the baseline (150 kW DAB booster, η 97 %, plus wide-input 800→48 V LLC, η 94 %, plus regulated 800→400 V converter, η 96.5 %): charging loss 4.5 → 0.5 kW; auxiliary 5 kW at 96.5 → 99.0 %; 48 V 3 kW at 94 → 96.8 %; magnetic VA ≈180 → ≈12.4 kVA; one device instead of two. Driving-cycle energy saving is small (≈56 Wh/100 km): the benefit is thermal (4 kW less heat during charging), volumetric and structural, not range.

Prior art. The fixed-ratio + partial-power principle is established [He 2018; Ni 2019; Xie 2025]; GM patents US 10363828/10632863 use a resonant switched-capacitor stage to feed a 400 V powertrain from an 800 V charger with output regulation; EP 3987646 combines a bidirectional 800/400 V converter with an APM through a transformer. PRISM-Hub differs in (a) direction and use—400 V charger to 800 V pack through an *unregulated* 2:1 stage with the regulator bypassed; (b) the isolated series cell with 200 V devices whose role changes per mode; (c) fixed-gain multiport isolation fed from the regulated bus; and (d) the four design rules. A full IEEE Xplore/patent search is reported in the supplementary material.

## VII. Conclusion

A single on-board hub can replace the booster/APM pair for 800 V vehicles if regulation is confined to a series partial-power cell and the bulk paths are fixed-ratio. The design is feasible at the simulation level; its critical engineering content is not the topology but the rules that make it work: self-oscillating ZCS with a blanking window, output-node stiffness, actively damped series-path control, and low-voltage derating. Hardware validation of the 2:1 array (ringing, thermal) is the next step.
